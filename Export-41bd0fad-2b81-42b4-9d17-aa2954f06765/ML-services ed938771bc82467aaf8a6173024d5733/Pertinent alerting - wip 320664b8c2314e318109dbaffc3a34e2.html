Introduction

There is a difference between precise anomaly detection and pertinent alerting: you can have precise anomaly detection but noisy alerting, for eg: drop of 60% on cpu usage that is normally at 2% usage. Though the drop is significant, the absolute value makes it worthless to alert on.

That said, the tuning of algorithms to have less false positives belongs to "precise anomaly detection", whereas, pertinent alerting is mainly hard coding (or getting user input) of the business knowledge about the data. Here we focus on the latter.

What anomalies are worthy of alerts?

Main parameters to determine the worthiness of generating alerts are:

  1. what metrics to alert on?
  1. at what granularity (scale)?
  1. duration, or how quickly to send an alert after detection of the anomaly?
  1. deviation
    1. percentage
    1. absolute value
  1. anomaly direction: up or down

(note that deviation and duration are also components of the anomaly detection algorithm itself, but additional business input is required for pertinent alerting)

1. what metrics to alert on?

refer to https://www.notion.so/packetai/UPIs-KPIs-and-important-metrics-per-technology-4b5f335969c845a59c18914dddd85489

2. at what granularity (aka time scale )?

Ideally, the AD engine would analyse each TS at multiple time scales (1min, 5min, 1h, 1d, 1w) - however, this would be costly to scale. To avoid huge scaling costs, we use domain knowledge about IT data to pick the scale of AD for different metrics.

→ this work is currently ongoing and the outcome would be a DB of metric/time_scale

3. duration, or how quickly to send an alert after detection?

Intuitively, anomalies that last longer are more important and hence worthier of generating alerts. The question is how "longer" is long enough, but also not too late? alerting after 5min could be too quick for some cases (eg. drop in user logins), since it could be just a passing behaviour that goes back to normal. On the other hand, alerting after 1 day on this metric would be too late.

→ we need to perform a metric classification. outcome is a DB of metric/time_to_alert

4. deviation

Deviation is the main component of an anomaly. However, deviation should be considered in a context: small deviation of a very large value could be significant whereas a large deviation from a normal small value could be ignorable in most cases (see example in the introduction). Thus, deviation should be considered in a context. The context could be determined by the metric business meaning or by its absolute value.

→ we need to augment metrics with contextual metadata

  • max absolute value
  • critical absolute value
  • critical deviation value

see https://www.anodot.com/blog/anodot-monitoring-and-alerting/

5. anomaly direction: up or down

Every anomaly is a deviation from a certain normal up or down. However, the anomaly direction may have a business meaning. Eg, latency down is mostly less worthy of the user attention than a latency up anomaly. To generalise, if the metric belongs to "more is better", then down anomalies are more important and vise versa.

→ we need to augment metrics with contextual metadata: "more is better" or "less is better".
