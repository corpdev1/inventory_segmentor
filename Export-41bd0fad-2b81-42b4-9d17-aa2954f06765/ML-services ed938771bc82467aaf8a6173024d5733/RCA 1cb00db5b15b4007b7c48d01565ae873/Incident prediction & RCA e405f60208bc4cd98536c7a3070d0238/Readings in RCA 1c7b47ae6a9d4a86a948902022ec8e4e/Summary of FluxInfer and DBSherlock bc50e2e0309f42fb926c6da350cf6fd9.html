Summary of FluxInfer and DBSherlock

"FluxInfer: Automatic Diagnosis of Performance Anomaly for Online Database System" from Dan Pei and collaborators

  • Good starting point; Leads to other papers.
  • It is based on an undirected graph approach where each node is a metric and they are fully connected. Given a detected anomalous time window, the time series of all the KPIs are considered for that window. The edges between KPIs are weighted based on a measure of correlation (Fisher Z test) and the most important nodes are inferred based on weighted PageRank algorithm.
  • By taking an undirected graph based approach, they are at least quantifying the top relevant features during an incident. Depending on the incident, the top features could be causal or symptomatic.
  • Their test set-up will be very useful for us.
    • OLTP-bench: https://github.com/oltpbenchmark/oltpbench

      Load generation for any JDBC compatible RDBMS.

    • https://kernel.ubuntu.com/~cking/stress-ng/

      Stress testing computer systems in various ways. Could be useful for us in various ways.

    • Many of their incidents are caused external to the DB using stress-ng, not because of heavy workload to the DB. We should see whether we can recreate similar situations. This will lead to a situation where the cause metric (CPU usage, memory usage) happens well ahead in time compared to the symptoms (DB metrics).
  • They also talk about metrics showing changes in a simultaneous manner (rather than some being much ahead of the others) in the context of databases.
  • According to DevOps engineers that they interviewed, the algorithm should take about 1 minute of time for it to be useful.

Please, check here for more information about experiments and observations and analysis about FluxInfer.

"DBSherlock: A Performance Diagnostic Tool for Transactional Databases" from Dong Young Yoon et al

  • A system based on user interaction.
  • Given an incident, anomalous and normal regions in the target KPI are either marked by the user or automatically.
  • The behavior of the various metrics during these time intervals are processed such that we have predicates to describe the behavior of the metrics. The predicates are in one of the three forms: X > a or X < b or a < X < b.
  • The user labels the incident by describing the root cause of the incident. Thus the system accumulates pairs of (root cause, set of predicates).
  • Given a new incident, all known root causes are ranked according to system's confidence level in each of them. The confidence is estimated based on the predicates associated with the root cause and the estimated predicates for the given incident.
  • As more and more of the same incident are happening, the predicates for the metrics can be merged.
  • It is a relatively simple system. The nice aspect is the user interface and the systematic approach to RCA in database systems (many sentences and phrases in the FluxInfer paper are directly taken from this paper). This is the paper that led to knowing about the DBSeer project.
