This formulation is also called l1l_1l1​﻿trend filtering. When the regularization term is based on l2l_2l2​﻿ norm (∥Dx∥22\left\Vert Dx \right\Vert_2^2∥Dx∥22​﻿), the formulation is called Hodrick-Prescott (H-P) filtering.

H-P filtering takes the form of a quadratic programme and it has a closed form solution. See page 341 of https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/l1_trend_filter.pdf.

One can notice that the output of H-P filtering is smooth compared to the output of l1l_1l1​﻿filtering. l1l_1l1​﻿ trend filtering is encouraged to fit piecewise linear segments to the time-series because of l1l_1l1​﻿ regularization. In practice, it works accordingly and fits piecewise linear segments. The boundaries between linear segments are related to changepoints. The last line segment could be linearly extended and used to make a prediction. However, in papers l1l_1l1​﻿ trend filtering doesn't seem to be directly used this way, it is used for preprocessing or as part of the formulation for another time-series analysis task.

What can trend analysis be used for?

Trend analysis is used a lot in economics, business etc. In a machine learning context, trend analysis helps with:

  • Preprocessing (mainly, denoising) time-series before they are passed to time-series analysis algorithms like prediction, change point detection, anomaly detection etc.
    • "Improved Predictive Deep Temporal Neural Networks with Trend Filtering", 2020. "On Robust Variance Filtering and Change of Variance Detection", 2020. Deep learning for short-term traffic flow prediction", 2017. "Short-term wind speed forecasting by spectral analysis from long-term observations with missing values", 2017.
  • Impute missing data (as preprocessing or as part of the formulation) in a time-series.
    • "Trend Filtering in Network Time Series with Applications to Traffic Incident Detection", 2017, "High-dimensional Time Series Prediction with Missing Values", 2016.

How to set λ\lambdaλ﻿ for l1l_1l1​﻿ trend filtering?

Apart from normalizing the data to zero mean and unit variance, the following points need to be considered:

  • What is the relationship between lambda and number of samples?
  • What is the relationship between lambda and level of noise in the time-series?

Definitely, λ\lambdaλ﻿ is affected by the number of samples.

smooth - MWA (1 hour): 10 days: λ\lambdaλ﻿ = 1000 for around 1500 samples (aggregation over 10 minutes) and λ\lambdaλ﻿ = 50 for around 500 samples (aggregation over 30 minutes) are comparable. Note: in some cases λ\lambdaλ﻿ = 200 for 500 samples works better.

smooth - MWA (1 hour): λ\lambdaλ﻿ = 1000 for 6 hours of data (2160 samples) is comparable to λ\lambdaλ﻿ = 50 for 2 hours of data (720 samples).

It looks like noise doesn't affect the output. It can be understood intuitively. Trend filtering aims to model the "average" signal, while being robust to noise.

Update: While testing with larger number of samples, λ\lambdaλ﻿ needs to be tweaked, so that the trend is not affected by local changes in the time-series. Please, check the page below titled "Interesting trends in Affluences data".

Some observations

  • If 10 second samples over a long period of time are taken (around 10 days, i.e., around 90000 samples), then the optimization solver exits without converging because of numerical error. This is with or without smoothing the time series with a moving window average (MWA) over 1 hour. This is for both l1l_1l1​﻿ and H-P filtering. Both the solvers CVXOPT and ECOS failed.
  • The optimization solver exits successfully, when the number of samples are reduced. 1. MWA samples over a period of 1 hour (yyy﻿ of length 360), 2. MWA followed by aggregation over a period of 10 minutes of the entire time-series (yyy﻿ of length 1497).
  • The regularization parameter (λ\lambdaλ﻿) needs to be adjusted to get the kind of trend that we want. In order to work with the same value of λ\lambdaλ﻿ across all time-series, it is important to normalize (zero-mean, unit variance) the time-series. So far, a value of 1000 looks reasonable.
