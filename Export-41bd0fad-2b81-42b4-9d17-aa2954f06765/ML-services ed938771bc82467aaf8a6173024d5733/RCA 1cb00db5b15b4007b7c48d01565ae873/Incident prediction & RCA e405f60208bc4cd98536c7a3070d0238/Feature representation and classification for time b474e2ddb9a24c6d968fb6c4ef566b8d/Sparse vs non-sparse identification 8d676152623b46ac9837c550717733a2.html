The approach

Experiments

In all the above experiments, a challenge has been to clearly label a time-series as sparse or not sparse in a given time window. In order to make more systematic progress in this regard, the following efforts are being undertaken.

Points requiring action

  • Need to clearly identify time-series that represent the classes of sparse and non-sparse. In other words, need to list a set of characteristics that can be readily used by a person to label to say something is sparse or not.
    • This is needed to make sure that we are able to catch the cases that cause problem for MAD but are difficult to identify.
  • Over what time windows will the time-series be analysed?

    Some time-series don't have the sparse spikes in a smaller time window and hence, don't appear sparse in that time window. So, do we fix a maximum possible time window for which we decide sparsity and categorize a time-series as being sparse if it appears sparse in that time window?

  • What is the size of the spike w.r.t. the range of the low values? If the spike is very small in size compared to the range of the low values, the spikes are just noise.

Observations

  • Removing the effect of trend is helpful to identify properties like sparse spikes. In general, it is an important preprocessing step while analysing time-series data. In our case, we are removing the trend based on exponential moving average.
  • Many time-series are made of spikes. Thus, at first glance a human observer tends to label most of the time-series as being sparse.
