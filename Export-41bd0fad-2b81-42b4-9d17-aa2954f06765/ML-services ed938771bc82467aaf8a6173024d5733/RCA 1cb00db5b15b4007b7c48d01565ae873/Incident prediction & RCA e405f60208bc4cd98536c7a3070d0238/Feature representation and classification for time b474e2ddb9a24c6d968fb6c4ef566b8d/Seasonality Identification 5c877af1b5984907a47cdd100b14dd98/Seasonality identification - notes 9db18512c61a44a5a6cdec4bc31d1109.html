Anodot's approach

Details from Anodot's KDD paper and white paper

Approach

  • Compute the autocorrelation function (ACF).
  • The duration between peaks of the ACF are clustered. The dominant cluster gives a very robust estimation of the period present in the series.

The autocorrelation function (ACF) has several advantages for seasonality detection:

  1. The local maximums of the ACF are integer multiples of the period of the time-series.
  1. The way ACF is computed naturally cancels noise.
  1. ACF is less sensitive to missing data.

Since the Fourier transform is a linear operator it propagate the noise and then the spectrum is noisy. In ACF, the noise - which is supposed to be independent of the signal - is automatically rejected. Thus, compared to the Fourier transform method, ACF is more accurate and less sensitive to missing data, but it is computationally expensive.

To reduce complexity we use a sampling technique for faster computation of the ACF. The sampling become now an exponential sampling. Note (after reading other papers): The autocorrelation is formally a convolution, and we can avoid the quadratic calculation in the time domain by computing it efficiently as a dot product in the frequency domain using the normalized Fourier transform.

Bank filter: The number and the definition of bands are based on statistical analysis of our data-set. In the system, we are computing the ACF for each band.

Often we see, not just a single seasonal pattern, but multiple seasonal patterns and even different types of multiple seasonal patterns. The different types of multiple seasonal patterns could happen as a multiplicative combination of signals (amplitude modulation) or additive combination of signals.

Comments by Anodot about related techniques: Two main approaches to pitch detection: Fourier spectrum based methods and variance matrix eigen analysis.

Other approaches based on ACF

"Characteristic-based clustering for time series data", Wang, Smith, Hyndman, 2006 (490 citations). Procedure to find time period:

  • Detrend time series using a regression spline with 3 knots.
  • Find r_k = Corr(Y_t, Y_{t−k}) (autocorrelation function) for all lags up to 1/3 of series length, then look for peaks and troughs in autocorrelation function.
  • Frequency is the first peak provided with following conditions: (a) There is also a trough before it; (b) The difference between peak and trough is at least 0.1; (c) The peak corresponds to positive correlation.
  • If no such peak is found, frequency is set to 1 (equivalent to non-seasonal). Some more notes from this paper:
  • Dealing with long time series.
  • When the time series is very long, some clustering algorithms may fail because the very notation of similarity is dubious in high dimension space.
  • Periodicity and seasonality are global characteristics.
  • Seasonal time series are sometimes also called periodic series although there is a major distinction between them. Periodicity (cyclical pattern) varies in frequency length, but seasonality has fixed length over each period. Periodicity is also referred as frequency in some literature.

Using both ACF and spectral density

"Robust Periodicity Detection Algorithms", Parthasarathy et al, 2006.

  • A wide-sense stationary random process z(t) is said to be be mean-square periodic, if ∃ T > 0 such that Rzz (τ) = Rzz (τ + T), ∀ τ, where Rzz (τ) is the autocorrelation function corresponding to z(t). The period of this process is then defined to be the smallest such T. The approach:
    • The spectrogram is constructed.
    • For each frequency, the evolution of the DFT coefficient with time is treated as a time-series. The ACF of each of these time-series are computed and averaged.
  • Paper also talks about multiple periods. Need to check whether the multiple periods combine multiplicatively or additively.

"On periodicity detection and structural periodic similarity" by Vlachos et al.

  • "A fully automated periodicity detection in time series" by Puech et al builds on this work.
  • There are two well known estimators of the PSD: the periodogram and the circular autocorrelation.
    • The periodogram P is provided by the squared length of each Fourier coefficient.
  • Due to the assumption of the Fourier Transform that the data is periodic, proper windowing of the data might be necessary for achieving a more accurate harmonic analysis. In this work we will sidestep this issue, since it goes beyond the scope of this paper. However, the interested reader is directed to ["On the Use of Windows for Harmonic Analysis with the Discrete Fourier Transform" by Harris, 1978] for an excellent review of data windowing techniques.
  • Issues with periodogram:
    • It is easy to see that the resolution of the periodogram becomes very coarse for longer periods.
    • The accuracy of the discovered periods, deteriorates for large periods, due to the increasing width of the DFT bins (N/k).
    • Another related issue is spectral leakage, which causes frequencies that are not integer multiples of the DFT bin width, to disperse over the entire spectrum. This can lead to ‘false alarms’ in the periodogram. However, the periodogram can still provide an accurate indicator of important short (to medium) length periods.
  • Defines autocorrelation without dividing by variance.
  • The autocorrelation is formally a convolution, and we can avoid the quadratic calculation in the time domain by computing it efficiently as a dot product in the frequency domain using the normalized Fourier transform. Approach:
    • use the periodogram for extracting period candidates.
    • assess the candidates using ACF.

Other points

  • Converting the values of the auto-correlation peaks from the time-domain to the frequency domain should result in the same peaks as the ones calculated by the FFT. The frequency of a signal thus can be found with the auto-correlation as well as with the FFT.
  • Power Spectral Density (PSD) is, in fact, the Fourier transform of the autocorrelation function.
  • The wavelet transform is better suited for analyzing signals with a dynamic frequency spectrum, i.e. the frequency spectrum changes over time, or has a component with a different frequency localized in time (the frequency changes abruptly for a short period of time).
  • Anodot is showing a confidence interval around a time series which models the seasonal behavior.
  • Issues like aliasing in the Fourier transform can cause multiple peaks to be present, some of which are not the actual seasonal frequency, but rather artifacts of the Fourier transform computation.
  • The analysis of the eigen values and eigen vectors of the variance matrix of the process. For stationary process the variance matrix is a Toepliz matrix built with the ACF vector.
