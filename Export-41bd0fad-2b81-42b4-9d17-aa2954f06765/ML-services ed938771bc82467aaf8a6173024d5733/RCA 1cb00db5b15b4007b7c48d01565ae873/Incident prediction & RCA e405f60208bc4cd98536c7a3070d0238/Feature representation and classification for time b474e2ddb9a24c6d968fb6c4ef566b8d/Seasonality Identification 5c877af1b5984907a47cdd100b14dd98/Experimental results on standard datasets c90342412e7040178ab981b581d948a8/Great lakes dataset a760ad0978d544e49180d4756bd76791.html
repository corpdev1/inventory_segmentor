The data can be downloaded from here (https://www.glerl.noaa.gov/data/dashboard/data/) by clicking on "Download all available data in a ZIP folder". The 2019 ACF+PSD paper is specifically looking at water levels, which can be found in the folder "GLDData/data/levels". That paper shows results for 2 lakes only. We have tested on 5 lakes.

The time-series are expected to have only one periodicity of 12 months. However, there are considerable up and down trends in all the time-series. This behavior is very much like the concept of cycles that is mentioned in time-series literature. Cycles are trends that go up and down but are not periodic in nature. The 2019 paper doesn't mention this. Not sure why. The ACF based Anodot approach is affected by this cycl

Some people talk about detrending the time-series before assessing the periodicity. This dataset seems to require that kind of preprocessing.

Ontario 1860 (58 years of data)

Anodot's ACF based approach

There is a dominant cluster corresponding to the time period of 12. There is a small cluster (of size 1) for the time period of 24, which is happening only because of the more global cyclic up and down behavior of the time-series.

ACF+PSD approach

The time period of 12 months is identified. Apart from there is a false positive of around 40 months.

Clair 1898 (20 years)

The 12 month period is alone identified, with no false positives.
