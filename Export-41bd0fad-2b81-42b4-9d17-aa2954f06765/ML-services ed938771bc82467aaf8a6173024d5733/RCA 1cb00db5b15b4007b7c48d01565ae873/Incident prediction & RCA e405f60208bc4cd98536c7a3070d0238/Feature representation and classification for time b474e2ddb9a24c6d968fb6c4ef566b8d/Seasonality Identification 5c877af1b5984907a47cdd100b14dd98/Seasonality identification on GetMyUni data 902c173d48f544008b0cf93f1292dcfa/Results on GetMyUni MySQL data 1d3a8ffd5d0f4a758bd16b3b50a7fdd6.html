The evolution of approaches based on ACF+PSD:

Approach 1. Approach according to the paper: cluster adjacent frequency candidates together and take only the average of the cluster as the candidate. Approach 2. Approach needed to correct miss in noisy periodic time series. For e.g., GetMyUni time-series like mysql#status#bytes#received-no_subject, mysql#status#bytes#sent-no_subject, mysql#status#threads#created-no_subject, mysql#status#connections-no_subject Note: The pseudo-periodic data set from UCR could be handled by Approach 1. So, in approach 2, we limit the size of the frequency candidate clusters to maximum size 2. Then, test the time-period candidates in ascending order of size, just like approach 1. In clusters of size 2, the average time period is taken as a candidate. Approach 3. Approach needed to correct miss in periodic sparse spikes with Approach 2. For e.g., GetMyUni time-series like mysql#status#handler#update-no_subject, mysql#status#handler#write-no_subject. In this approach, we test all frequency candidates proposed by the power spectral density. Though, we cannot do it directly in one loop. We first identify clusters based on Approach 2. We choose the first element of all the clusters as candidate clusters. We test the candidates in ascending order of time period, just like Approach 1. Then, we take the second elements (if present) of all the clusters and test them in ascending order, just like approach 1.

Some observations:

  • Approach 3 picks the 1 day period in almost all the cases. The only miss is in the case of mysql#status#open#tables-no_subject. Most likely the reason is the huge anomalous spike, which is compromising the magnitude of the rest of the time-series. A test by removing this spike will be done soon.
  • 1 day time period in very noisy time-series are also identified.
  • Apache has 10 sparse time-series out of 15. On very noisy cases it is difficult to identify the 1 day period.
