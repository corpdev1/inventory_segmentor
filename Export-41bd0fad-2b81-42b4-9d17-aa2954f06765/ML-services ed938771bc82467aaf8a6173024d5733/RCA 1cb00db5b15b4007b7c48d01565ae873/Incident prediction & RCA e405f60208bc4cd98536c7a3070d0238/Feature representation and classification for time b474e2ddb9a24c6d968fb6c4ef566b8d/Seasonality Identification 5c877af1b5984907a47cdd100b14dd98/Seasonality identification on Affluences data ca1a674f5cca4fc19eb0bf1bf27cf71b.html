Below, you will find separate pages for each component. Within each page, you will find the time-series grouped according to the algorithm's output (time-period detected, not detected etc). Each of the groups has a corresponding page, in which the plots are available.

Note: In the plots, the x values are the samples, where each sample corresponds to a period of 10 seconds. Thus, 8640 samples corresponds to 86400 seconds or 24 hours.

Note: The y values of the plots are the values of the metrics. First, they are obtained from Metricbeat and aggregated over 10 second periods. Next, a moving window average over a period of 24 hours is @ computed for each time step and that is what is used for seasonality analysis.

Observations

  • Most of the periodic time-series have only one time-period of 24 hours.
  • All the period detection misses in Docker-1 and Docker-2 are because of those cases having a strong trend and considerable amplitude variation. Amplitude variation is relatively common in time-series coming from IT infrastructure and our algorithm handles it well. However, trend along with amplitude variation is challenging even for a human observer. Please, take a look at those time-series by navigating through the pages below.
  • In Linux-3, in the multi-period time series, even though the period of 64800 seconds is missed, a period of 56290 seconds is still detected in these cases (which is currently marked as a false positive).
  • In Linux-4, there is considerable noise in the two time-series where the period is missed. Even though the daily period (86400 seconds) is missed. There is a close false positive of 100570 seconds.

  • RabbitMQ metrics are very noisy: This is leading to both false positives and misses.
  • A few of the false positives in the MySQL metrics are because of the appearance of a smaller peak between the larger periodic peaks. Probably, these time-series should be labelled as multi-periodic and these false positives should be treated as true positives.
  • False positives are generally because of noise. Some of the false positives can be avoided by setting a threshold on the peak of the autocorrelation function (ACF) (> 0.1 looks like a reasonable choice) and a threshold on the detected periodicity (> (1/20) or (1/30) of the total length used for analysis).
