Experiments with GMU data: after MWA

@Hariprasad Kannan wrote:

All the time-series in this experiment were pre-processed with a moving window average (MWA) over 1 hour. Such a smoothing is needed for periodicity analysis.

After further experiments with Affluences data for sparse vs non-sparse identification, it looks like raw data is preferable for this task. Nevertheless, testing with smooth data gave better understanding about how the approach works.

96 time-series have been tested. 41 are classified as sparse. 55 as non-sparse.

16 of the time-series that are classified as sparse have been noted down as false positives. They are labeled as false positives in a somewhat strict manner. Please, take a look at the time-series and share your thoughts. Most likely, many of them are true positives.

2 of the time-series marked as non-sparse have been marked as false negatives. Again, take a look at the time series and share your thoughts.

Below, True and False are the labels given by the approach. The false positives and misses have been noted at the end of each set.

You will find the time-series in a zip file for each set. Just look at the left most figure. These plots were made for the seasonality identification project.
