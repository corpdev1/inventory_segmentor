Summary

  • We are mainly interested in time-series that are seasonal, sparse, step, step with noise, only white noise.
  • Seasonal time-series belonging to different time periods, are seen as belonging to separate classes by a multi-class classifier. Also, a seasonal time-series could be sparse or step shaped or made of values that are periodic globally but are high frequency locally.
  • Thus, an important concern is how to classify time series belonging to unseen classes. The time series classification task can be addressed by a system, which is made of separate and interacting approaches: one of the approaches will handle seasonality identification, others will handle sparse and step shaped time-series.
  • There is a very robust approach that can identify whether a time series belongs to the category of (sparse or step) or others. The approach could be further extended to differentiate between sparse (with concept shift) and step.

Experiment with synthetic data

Synthetic samples of the following types were created and Rocket was trained on them:

  • Sparse: low magnitude white noise, with occasional spikes.
  • Seasonal: differing amplitudes, phase and time period.
  • Step: Flat regions with occasional jump between flat regions.

100 samples of each type are generated and the train/test split is roughly 50-50. All the time series in a given class are given the same label.

Observations with synthetic data

  • Multiclass classification using Rocket leads to almost 100% accuracy, when the set of seasonal time series are:
    • All having the same time period.
    • The training and test sets see samples from all possible time periods
  • However, when the test set contains a time series with a period that is not seen during training, then that time series usually gets misclassified either as sparse or as step.
  • Thus, misclassification of unseen classes needs to be properly handled.

Handling seasonal data and unseen classes, in general

One way to handle misclassification of unseen classes is to work with classifiers that output a confidence score for each class label and to train one-vs-all classifiers for each of the known classes. An unseen class will get a poor score from all these classifiers.

  • Ridge classifier of scikit-learn handles multi-class classification based on one-vs-all classifiers and outputs confidence scores for each of the class. More investigation is needed to interpret these scores when input is an unseen class.

Sparse and step shape classification

Another way to handle misclassification of unseen time series, is to have a set of approaches, each of which is strong in identifying certain classes.

For sparse signals, observing the histogram is a strong way to judge whether a time series is made of sparse spikes or not. If we divide the values into histograms and observe a large fraction of the values in the histogram bins with lower value ranges, then almost surely the time series is sparse.

For step shaped time-series, the original time-series will not be sparse, while the first-order difference time-series will be sparse. This is true for step shaped time-series with noise also. This is a strong way to identify step shaped time-series.

These approaches should work side by side with another approach that identifies whether a time-series is seasonal. A seasonal time-series could be sparse or step shaped or made of values that are periodic globally but are high frequency locally. Also, seasonality identification is linked with identifying the time period of the time-series. We have observed that time-series with time periods that are unseen during training can be misclassified. Thus, an approach for seasonality identification should work side by side with approaches for identifying sparse or step shaped. A time-series that is none of these (seasonal, sparse, step shaped), is almost surely white noise.

The problem with the above approach is that sparse time-series with a concept shift could be classified as step shaped time-series.

There are a few ways to deal with this:

  • Train a classifier (like Rocket) to discriminate between step shaped and sparse with concept shift.
  • Look at the area under the curve representing the time-series. After normalizing the range of minimum to maximum values of a time-series between 0 to 1, we can see that a time-series that is sparse with concept shift will have lesser area under the curve, than a time-series that is made of only steps.

    In a sparse with concept shift time series, the presence of the spikes will limit the level of the concept shift. Thus, the area under the curve will be lesser.

  • Train a classifier which is based on features that capture local shapes better. Rocket, even though is based on kernels which act locally, the features for each kernel is based on global pooling (max and percentage of positive values).
