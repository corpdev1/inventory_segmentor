  • More than 90% of the anomalies are in the form of up and down level shifts or up and down spikes.
  • The exceptional anomalies are:
    • Sparse spikes (Mainly, disk I/O. Maybe, CPU, also. Usually, CPU metric is made of spikes and plateaus).

A sparse time series has spikes once in a while. Sparse anomalies are also spikes, whose peak value is different from other normal spikes in the time series.

Among the metrics that have level shift or spike, the shape of the normal behavior can have either low or high level of noise. It will be a good idea to assess the effect of this noise on MAD performance.

Now, we will see that an accurate identification of level shift and spike can help with many (probably, 90%) of the metrics.

In all of the following examples, the anomaly is a change in the level. In the case of the MySQL metrics questions (discrete variable with a lot of possible values so can be considered as continuous) and connections (discrete variable with a limited number of possible values. Default maximum: 150), the change is a reduction in the number of questions and number of connections, when the anomaly happens. In the case of the RabbitMQ metric, the change is an increase in the level.

We could consider the MySQL cases to be very noisy signals compared to the RabbitMQ signal. Please, see the above sets of metrics to notice these cases happening across many metrics. Thus we have a problem where we need to detect level shifts (and spikes) in a manner that is robust to noise.
