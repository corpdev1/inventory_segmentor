Early incident detection - Customer story - ready

Growth results in more complexity

Affluences is a fast growing navigation app that allows users to get directions, estimate waiting time and occupancy rate of attractions such as museums, sport facilities etc. Recently, Affluences has been experiencing a fast growth of its app usage so the team has invested significantly in growing and modernising their IT stack, which as a result, has become increasingly dynamic and more complex making it more difficult to monitor and manage.

Proactive observability is necessary to reduce growth pains

Affluences uses a traditional monitoring tool that relies on setting thresholds and rule-based manual alerts, which so far has been very useful for them to detect significant issues. However, with the rapid growth and complexity, Affluences was in the look for additional tools to improve on their incident detection capabilities and become more proactive.

Because of the real time nature of Affluences' business, it is needless to say that an incident that is not detected in real time could have a deep negative impact on their users experience, and ultimately on their business and brand.

Experiencing PacketAI

Affluences was one of PacketAI's first beta testers and we have a particularly close relationship with them. They decided to setup the PacketAI solution after seeing our demo and understanding how such a tool could amplify their capability to deal with infrastructure issues. In the following, we will detail an incident story where PacketAI helped Affluences to early-detect an impactful incident on their production infrastructure.

On a Thursday to Friday night, a silent issue happened without generating any threshold alerts. The incident started as a usual reboot of a VM (let's call it VM1), which would at most generated a low severity notification or some reboot logs. After the reboot however, other neighbouring (load balanced) VMs started experiencing issues.

"We indeed had a silent incident during the night. We suspect the hypervisor running the VM encountered an issue and rebooted the VM. Docker on this instance failed to acquire the proper vlan IP after reboot and was unable to accept new containers from the cluster"

Rapahael Galmiche, head of infrastructure, Affluences

This issues were not captured by thresholds and no alert from traditional monitoring tools were generated.

However, on the PacketAI side, our AI algorithms generated anomaly alerts on the rebooted VM (let's call it VM1) starting at 00:52 (figure 1), as it did not go back to its normal behaviour (it switched to a low activity because of the failure to attribute proper IP addresses), as measured across multiple metrics (figure 2 and figure 3).

Because Affluences infrastructure runs on a dynamic docker environment, many services are deployed across multiple hosts. As a result, load balanced VMs (see VM2 activity on figure 2 for example) started experiencing higher load.

After around 10minutes from PacketAI first alerts, traditional threshold tools started sending “CPU > 85%” alerts on neighbouring VMs and these alerts lasted until a human intervention from the Affluences team.

What's interesting in this case is difference between PacketAI generated alerts vs traditional threshold alerts:

Traditional threshold alert:

  • 53 threshold alerts (on cpu usage metric on 3 hosts)
  • time of first generated alert: 01:01

AI generated alerts:

  • count: 10 on 6 different components
  • time of first generated alert: 00:51
  • a few of these anomalies indicated the root cause (docker service perf metrics) from where the Affluences team understood and fixed the problem

These qualities of AI generated alerts is what our customers are looking for to complement their monitoring practices, both in terms of reducing manual work of creating and maintaining manual threshold alerts and becoming more proactive by spotting small issues before they snowball into big business-impacting incidents.

Although it is a new product, PacketAI solution impressed us many times at finding small problematic signals before we (humans) or our existing rule-based tools noticed them. As we scale, this will be crucial for us during these growth phases as risks of silent incidents is higher, and stakes are also higher in terms of our reputation and continuity of service. Because of this and new coming features around logs, we will potentially use PacketAI as our default observability tool.

Micaël Pais Novo, CTO, Affluences
