Relevance score

In order to derive a relevance score formula based on data we will rely on the following properties of anomalies: duration of anomalous period, deviation from the "normal mean" and time between two anomalies.

Why these properties?

Before diving into formula derivation, let's understand what these three properties could bring to us.

  • Duration shows for how long the anomalous period lasted. Intuitively, anomalies with longer duration should be scored higher.
  • Deviation measures how far the observed value is from the overall average
  • Time between two anomalies will determine the distribution of time that passes between anomalies. This property could help to silence anomalies which arise periodically and highlight the ones that are out of the ordinary.

Tradeoff between deviation, duration and time between

We need then to find the tradeoff between these three variables based on business objectives and common sense.

From the Anodot paper:

"Consider the outliers shown in Figure 1b. Even without looking at the assigned numbers for some of the outliers, a person looking at the signal would probably come up with similar scores. How? It is not based on each outlier’s amount of deviation from normal, rather, it is based on the fact that the high peak outliers deviated a lot more, and the smaller ones deviated less than the bigger ones. Even for human eyes, it is all relative."

From a human perspective, that when people look at a long history of a time series and see the outliers within it, in their minds they consider the outliers relative to each other as well as relative to normal.

Thus, we assume that:

  1. Large deviation anomalies will be scored higher
  1. Short anomalous periods with a small deviation from the mean are perceived more as noise than an actual anomaly and should have a lower anomaly score
  1. Anomalies that occur periodically and have a commensurate deviation should be scored lower with each new occurrence

Formula for relevance score

Based on the assumptions above and explored distributions for deviation, duration and time between anomalies we could end up with the following formula:

score=deviation_significance∗w1+duration_significance∗w2+time_between_significance∗w3w1+w2+w3score = \frac{deviation\_significance * w_1 + duration\_significance * w_2 + time\_between\_significance * w_3}{w_1+w_2+w_3}score=w1​+w2​+w3​deviation_significance∗w1​+duration_significance∗w2​+time_between_significance∗w3​​﻿

where weights w1,w2,w3w_1, w_2, w_3w1​,w2​,w3​﻿ show the significance of deviation, duration and time between relative to each other.

For example if we choose the following weights:

weights = {
		"deviation": 3,
		"duration": 1,
		"tb": 2,
}

this will mean that 50% of the final score will represent deviation of received value from the overall average, 17% - duration of abnormal period and 33% - time between two anomalies.

Deviation

Finally, let's take a look on the deviation distribution. As before we first start with brute-force fit.

This time we do not have a dominant distribution for our data. Different metrics showed different behaviour as well as performed statistical tests. Some of them showed that the best possible fit for observed data could be either log-normal or inverse Gaussian distribution, other showed that exponential or beta distributions suit the best.

The simplest way to understand the significance of observed value is to take a look on the empirical distribution function which, as we know, is an estimate of the cumulative distribution function that generated the points in the sample.

Experiments

Score definition

For this experiment we will calculate relevance scores as follows:

Duration

We will be using the following rule-based significance formula:

if current_duration > 12:
		duration_significance = 1.0
elif current_duration > 6:
		duration_significance = 0.8
elif current_duration > 3:
		duration_significance = 0.6
elif current_duration > 1:
		duration_significance = 0.4
else:
		duration_significance = 0.2

Since we are dealing with 10-second data,

  • the duration of 1 (represents 10 seconds) will be assigned with a score of 0.2
  • the duration between 2 and 3 (represents 20s - 30s) will be assigned with a score of 0.4
  • the duration between 4 and 6 (represents 40s - 1 min) will be assigned with a score of 0.6
  • the duration between 7 and 12 (represents 1 - 2 min ) will be assigned with a score of 0.8
  • the duration of 12 and more (represents 2 min and more) will be assigned with a score of 1

Deviation

Empirical distribution function from statsmodel package will be used to define the significance score.

Time between

We will use log-normal distribution to model the time between the two anomalies and calculate the significance score. More precisely, if viv_ivi​﻿ is the time between anomaly i−1 and ii-1 \text{ and } ii−1 and i﻿ we will be calculating the z-score of log(vi)log(v_i)log(vi​)﻿

Conclusion

We have established an approach to score anomalies regarding their importance. During this project we studied different properties of anomalies concentrating ourselves mostly on the following three of them: the deviation from the normal average, the duration of the abnormal period and the time between observed anomalies.

Statistical tests were performed to assess the goodness of fit with theoretical distributions and different strategies were tried to derive individual significance scores. We have seen, that while the time between two anomalies is well approximated by log-normal distribution, other properties need individual studies and the simplest way to measure the importance of deviation is taking the values of the empirical distribution function and the simple rule-based score for the duration of abnormal period.

Finally, we presented 3 examples of usage of relevance score where we demonstrate how it could possibly help to reduce the storm of false alarms and find the most significant anomalies based on the underlying distributions.
