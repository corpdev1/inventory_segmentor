Relevance score experiment

@Hariprasad Kannan wrote:

The main concerns to be addressed are:

  • Choice of the probability distribution, especially when there is limited data.
    • Geometric distribution is a reasonable solution.
  • For higher probability events the score should not be reduced to a very low value.
    • Compare log probabilities.
  • Measuring the duration of the anomaly.

Of the three, measuring the duration will lead to better pay-off. It is more challenging, also.

Anodot fits a probabilistic distribution on past anomalies. Then for a new anomaly, its score is adjusted according to this distribution.

Anodot recommends fitting an exponential distribution to a set of features describing the anomalies. Deviation and duration are the foremost. Deviation itself could be of two types (relative with respect to other anomalies and absolute with respect to the normal behavior). Another feature mentioned is volatality. Otherwise, they mention this phrase "other conditions around both duration and deviation".

We have so far modeled based on two variables: duration and deviation.

Anodot talks about fitting an exponential model. Generally exponential distributions are univariate. Bivariate (and multivariate, in general) exponential distributions exist but they are a research topic and stable software packages don't seem to be there. So, to fit a bivariate distribution in our case, we treat the two variables as being independent and fit univariate distributions to each variable.

At the end of the experiment, we have concluded that the geometric distribution is practical for us.

To reach this conclusion we tested the following four distributions:

  • Poisson.
  • Exponential.
  • Negative binomial.
  • Geometric.

Our variables:

  • Duration: discrete in nature, especially, we are aggregating to 10 second windows.
    • Experimented with: Poisson, Negative binomial, Geometric.
  • Deviation: Continuous in nature. Could be discretized to buckets.
    • Experimented with: Exponential, Geometric.

Data

  • Synthetic.
  • CPU metric.

Poisson

  • Poisson distributions are used to model count data. It models the number of times an event can happen in a given period of time. e.g., the number of typos in a book with a given total number of words, the number of arrivals of patients in a hospital on a given day.
    • In our case: When an anomaly happens, how long will it be?
  • However, Poisson distribution has a limited variance. Since, for a Poisson random variable,E[X]=λ      var(X)=λ\bold{E}[X]=\lambda \;\;\; \text{var}(X) = \lambdaE[X]=λvar(X)=λ﻿, where λ\lambdaλ﻿ is called the rate parameter.

    It is not heavy tailed. Thus,

    • if there is a huge range of values in the samples
    • if there are limited number samples with a huge range in values.

    then the fitted model shifts the mode of the Poisson, i.e., λ\lambdaλ﻿ increases to a large value.

    For e.g., durations = [9, 10, 29, 139, 178], we get λ=73.13\lambda=73.13λ=73.13﻿.

    Probabilities = 9 (2.8723644232064745e-21), 10 (2.1004773003315053e-20), 29 (2.254409587432086e-09), 139 (2.3185666291896908e-12), 178 (1e-128)

  • Possible solutions:
    • Ignore "outliers". Fitting Poisson by ignoring outliers is a topic of research
      • Difficult to decide what to ignore in our case.
    • Fit Poisson after applying log transformation on the duration.
      • Difficult to interpret.

Negative binomial

  • The number of successes in a sequence of independent and identically distributed Bernoulli trials before a specified (non-random) number of failures (denoted by rrr﻿) occurs. For example, we can define rolling a 6 on a die as a failure, and rolling any other number as a success, and ask how many successful rolls will occur before we see the third failure (r=3r=3r=3﻿).
    • In our case: How long will the anomaly last, before a normal sample is obtained?
  • Allows for more variance (overdispersion).
  • Similar to Poisson, the mode is away from 000﻿.

Exponential

  • Amount of time before an event happens. e.g., the amount of time until a piece of equipment breaks down, until a light bulb burns out or until an accident occurs.
  • Nice property: Heavy tail.
    • Works reasonably when the sample size is limited.
  • Intuitively, models lesser deviation and lesser duration with higher probability. Not clear, how to interpret a distribution with a mode away from 000﻿.
  • Meant for continuous random variables. fX(x)f_X(x)fX​(x)﻿ is the probability density function, not the probability.
  • Not a problem, if we are taking comparing two probabilities as a ratio. But not suitable for looking at log probability.
  • Log probability is needed to deal with huge range of values for the variable. So that higher probability events don't get squished.

Geometric

  • Discrete analog of the exponential.
  • Heavy tailed.
  • Comparison of log probabilities is possible.
  • The deviation needs to be discretized into buckets.
  • The approach cannot handle multi-modal data. For our practical purposes it is ok.

Training samples

  • How many are sufficient?
    • Experiments with synthetic Poisson, showed a minimum size of around 25.
    • Geometric (and exponential) can give reasonable fit with less data itself.
  • When to retrain?

Measuring the duration

Why is measuring the duration important?

  • Assign score in a better manner to anomalies which are short but greater than 1 unit.
    • Maintain the anomaly score better during the anomaly.
    • Handle the situation where a return to normal is seen as an anomaly.
  • Recognize long anomalies and ask the user whether it is a long anomaly or a concept shift.

  • We will also keep track of the actual values happening during this time. We will use them once the anomaly is over.

Is the geometric distribution stable?

Note: The following results are equally applicable to using CDF of exponential distribution. The geometric distribution is the discrete counterpart of the exponential.

As mentioned in the beginning of this page, the most important tasks are measuring the duration (and based on the duration, the deviation) of an anomaly. Once these values are in hand for an anomaly, any monotonically decreasing distribution will be good enough.

As an extreme approach: Even a linear interpolation between the largest and smallest values may be good enough. For e.g., we can maintain a sliding window of the past N anomalies and see the smallest and largest values separately for duration and deviation and assign a score for new anomalies based on this scale.

Some plots that show that the geometric distribution is a stable fit for getting relevance score.

  • Keeping deviation constant and trying different values for deviation (500 samples).
  • Keeping duration constant and trying different values for duration (500 samples).

We can see that higher deviation or higher duration lead to lesser probability (y axis is log probability).
