The problems that are happening in the above cases could be addressed by the following:

  • We are applying MP in a streaming manner. Thus, the final scores that we use for anomaly detection are different (there is a normalization w.r.t. maximum MP score) from the scores that we get by applying MP in a non-streaming manner.
  • There is noise in the time-series that affects MP computation. MWA based smoothing helps.
    • Refer lfanalyzer_6040a96cdcc614001153fc55-docker-log-nodeapp_app-web-stashed regarding the effect of noise. Without smoothing MP anomaly score is high at many places for noisy time-series.
  • There are low values of the raw MP score that need to be filtered out. This helps with better computation of the streaming score.
  • When a window is larger, it most likely will match with a smaller set of other windows. Similarly, when a window is smaller, it could match with more number of other windows. In a periodic time-series, especially with sparse periodic spikes, if the window is smaller than the time-period, the following happens. Windows between the spikes are made of noise and could be matched over a broad range of other such windows and achieve a very low MP score. However, the windows containing the spikes could be matched with a much smaller set of other windows and get relatively larger MP score. Thus, when the window size is less than the time period, one or more of the windows containing the periodic spikes are marked as anomalies by our streaming algorithm. Thus, it is important to consider window sizes larger than the detected time period.
  • There are situations where the MP score is low but not very low and the window still matches well with many previous windows. This happens because of noise. A logic needs to be implemented to deal with this. It is described here.

  • Apart from choosing a window larger than the detected time period (see discussion above), here is no consistent rule that leads us to a suitable MP window.
