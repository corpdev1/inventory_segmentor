Description

  • Logram finds dynamic and static tokens by identifying low occuring bi-grams and tri-grams.
  • Given a set of logs, the set of all bi-grams and tri-grams are first identified. If the bi-grams and tri-grams are identified from individual logs, the beginning and ending tokens will be represented less in the bi-grams and tri-grams and could be mistaken to be dynamic because of low occurrence. Hence, they concatenated all the logs into one very big sentence and then identify the bi-grams and tri-grams.
  • First, the low occurring tri-grams are identified based on a threshold. Among these tri-grams, the low occurring bi-grams are identified. In these low occurring bi-grams, those tokens that shared by two overlapping bi-grams are labelled as dynamic. The rest of the tokens are labelled as static.
  • There could be a special situation where a static token is surrounded by two dynamic tokens and the tri-gram and bi-grams are low occurring. In other words, the low occurring tri-gram is of the form token1-token2-token3 and suppose the bi-grams token1-token2 and token2-token3 are also low occurring. In this case, token2 in the middle could be mis-identified as being dynamic, as it is the token shared by two low-occurring bi-grams. In this case (low occuring tri-gram made of two low occurring bi-grams), a pseudo tri-gram of the form ?-token2-? is created. If token2 is static then this tri-gram will have high occurrence and we can figure out that this token is static. If token2 is dynamic then this pseudo tri-gram will have low count.

    Note: This concept is not explained clearly in the paper.

Thresholds for identifying the low occurring tri-grams and bi-grams

The paper describes a procedure based on the 2D plot of the following two quantities:

  • Each n-gram occurs a particular number of times in the entire data-set. These occurrence counts across all the n-grams form the x values.
  • For each occurrence count, the total number of n-grams occurring that many times form the y values.

For e.g., if in a small toy data-set, there are 3 bi-grams occurring 1 time, 4 bi-grams occurring 2 times and 1 bi-gram occurring 5 times, then x = [1, 2, 5] and y = [3, 4, 1].

A smooth curve is fit through these points and the derivative is computed for points on this curve. These derivative values are clustered into two groups using a 1D k-means algorithm. Based on these two clusters the threshold point is set.

The intuition for this approach seems to be the following: The number of n-grams with a given occurrence count is expected to decrease as the occurrence count increases. By fitting a smooth curve to the observed data, the knee of this curve is used to set the threshold. This knee point is recognized as the point that separates the steep part of the curve (large derivative values) from the more flat part.

We can in see the below plots that the smooth curve doesn't capture the steep decrease in the y values in the initial stages. With decreasing smoothness the fitted curve has too much variability and the derivative based threshold will not work.

You can observe this by zooming to the bottom left part of the below plots. These plots are based on the bigrams. Plots for different values of the smoothing window are shown.

Window = 0.1 (fraction of the data used to smooth the y value)

Window = 0.66 (default)

One possible work around to this situation, is to compute the cumulative value of the y values and look for the knee in the resulting curve, as shown below. The curve cumulative values is bound to have a monotonic shape. Refer the function getNGramOccurrences in DictionarySetUp.py. It uses the package https://github.com/arvkevi/kneed

Results

When Logram was tested with hard coded thresholds of 10 and 15 (as given in the code shared by the authors), 84739 templates were identified by the algorithm.

When we look at the templates, we can see that many unique templates were identified because of unique values in the IP address and port number. We will see later results with masking.

When we apply the knee based threshold on the unmasked data, we get 23 templates, of which one is made of only wild cards.

The knee based thresholds are: Bigrams: 99. Trigrams: 96.

Observations

  • When applied directly on the logs, Logram gives reasonable results and could be competitive with Drain.
    • Drain is somewhat better in the particular case above.
  • Its performance is sensitive to the two threshold parameters.
  • However, it will lead to undesirable collapsing together of templates if it is applied to the output of Drain.
    • We have observed collapsing together of templates when Logram is applied on either Drain output (with masking) or the masked logs, with the knee based thresholds.
